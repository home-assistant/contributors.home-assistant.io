Home Assistant is available in 64 languages, and every one of them is translated by people who use it. When someone in Brazil, Japan, or Finland opens Home Assistant for the first time, the words they see were written by a volunteer like you.

You do not need to know how to code to help. If you are a native speaker and you care about how your language reads, you have everything it takes.

## Two ways to help

There are two kinds of translation work, and they use different tools:

- **Interface text** is everything you read on screen: menus, settings, dialogs, error messages, and the names of entities and their states. You translate it in your browser, in [Home Assistant's projects on Lokalise](/translate/first-contribution/#1-pick-a-project).
- **Voice commands** are the sentences Home Assistant understands when you talk to Assist, and the answers it gives back. They live in YAML files on GitHub, and language leaders review the changes for their language. See [Voice](/translate/voice/).

Most people start with interface text. It takes a few minutes to get going, and what you translate ships without a review step, see [how translations get back](/translate/how-it-works/#how-translations-get-back).

## Who to talk to

- Questions about a specific string? Leave a comment on it in Lokalise. Other translators of your language see it there.
- Missing a language, or want to discuss how translation works? Use the [localization discussions](https://github.com/home-assistant/frontend/discussions/categories/localization) on GitHub.
- Working on voice commands? The [intents discussions](https://github.com/OHF-Voice/intents/discussions) are the place, and the language leader of your language reviews your changes. Language leaders meet in the **#language-leaders** channel on the [Home Assistant Discord](https://www.home-assistant.io/join-chat/).
