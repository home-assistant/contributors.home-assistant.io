Your home is not always instant. A device takes a moment to answer, history has to be fetched, a backup takes minutes. A good wait tells people that something is happening, and where the result will show up.

## When to use

Show a loading state whenever people would otherwise look at a screen that seems stuck or empty. Pick the indicator by where the wait happens:

- **A whole area loads, and you don't know how long it takes**: a [spinner](/design/components/spinner/).
- **A button started an action that runs on the spot**: the button shows that it is busy.
- **The action finishes in place and has nowhere else to show its result**: a [progress button](/design/components/progress-button/).
- **Content loads into a layout you already know**, like rows of a table or the text of a tile: a skeleton, a grey placeholder in the shape of the content.
- **An operation has several steps**: show progress through the steps, so people know how far along it is.

## How it works

### Spinner

The spinner comes in four sizes: `tiny` (16 pixels), `small` (28), `medium` (48, the default), and `large` (68).

### Busy buttons

A [button](/design/components/button/) with `loading` shows a spinner instead of its label and can't be selected again, so the action does not start twice. The confirmation dialogs of the frontend do this with their main button while the action runs.

A progress button goes one step further. When the action is done, it shows a check mark on a green button or an alert icon on a red one, for two seconds. It says *that* something failed, not *why*. When people need to know why, also show an [error](/design/patterns/errors/).

  <ha-progress-button progress>Restart</ha-progress-button>
</Demo>

### Skeletons

A skeleton keeps the layout in place while the content loads, so nothing jumps around when it arrives. The frontend has `ha-skeleton`, with `ha-skeleton-text` and `ha-skeleton-icon` for the common shapes. Data tables, tiles, and the devices dashboard use them.

### Fading in

Loading indicators fade in rather than pop up, but not all at the same pace:

- **A skeleton** fades in straight away, over about 300 milliseconds. It stands in for the content, so waiting would leave a gap where the layout should be.
- **A spinner or progress bar** waits half a second before it fades in, or a full second for a whole page. Most loads finish sooner, and then people never see a spinner flash on and off.
- **The content itself** appears as soon as it arrives, without a fade.

### Where loading ends

A loading state always ends in one of three places: the content, an [empty state](/design/patterns/empty-states/), or an [error](/design/patterns/errors/). Never leave a spinner running when the request failed, and never show "nothing here" while the data is still on its way.

Dashboard cards follow the same rule. Every card handles loading, an error, an unavailable entity, and an entity that does not exist.

## Accessibility

- The spinner has the role of a progress bar, with a translated "Loading" label. Give a group of skeletons the same role and label, rather than visible "Loading" text.
- Skeletons and spinners keep their meaning without motion. Skeletons stop pulsing when people ask for reduced motion. See [Motion](/design/foundations/motion/).
- When loading ends in an error, show it as text people can read, not only as a red button.

## In code

```ts
html`
  <ha-spinner size="small"></ha-spinner>
  <ha-button .loading=${this._saving}>Save</ha-button>
  <ha-progress-button .progress=${this._running}>
    Restart
  </ha-progress-button>
`;
```

When the action of a progress button is done, call `actionSuccess()` or `actionError()` on it. The skeletons, `ha-skeleton`, `ha-skeleton-text`, and `ha-skeleton-icon`, live in `src/components/skeleton/`.

Wrap a loading indicator in `ha-fade-in`, from `src/components/animation/`, to fade it in:

```ts
html`
  <ha-fade-in .duration=${300} easing="ease-in">
    <div role="progressbar" aria-label=${localize("ui.common.loading")}>
      <ha-skeleton-text></ha-skeleton-text>
    </div>
  </ha-fade-in>
  <ha-fade-in .delay=${500}><ha-spinner></ha-spinner></ha-fade-in>
`;
```
