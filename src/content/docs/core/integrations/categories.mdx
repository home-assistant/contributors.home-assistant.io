Integration categories are how people find integrations: on the integrations page of the website, in the Home Assistant app, and in the Marketplace. An integration can list one to three of them in the `categories` key of its [manifest](/core/integrations/manifest/#categories). The key is optional for now.

## How to choose

Ask yourself one question: what is this, and where would someone look for it? A category describes the product or service, not the entities Home Assistant creates for it.

1. Pick the primary category: the one that says what your integration is for. A thermostat brand is Climate, even when it also creates sensors and switches.
2. Use a subcategory when one clearly fits, like `climate.thermostats_heating`. Otherwise, use the category on its own.
3. Add a second or third category only when your integration really is that too, in a way people would browse for. Shelly makes smart plugs and switches, but also bulbs and energy meters, so it lists all three.

Most integrations need one category. Some guidance for the cases that come up often:

- A hub or bridge that brings in many kinds of devices, like Zigbee or a multi-purpose vendor bridge, goes in Hubs & protocols. A bridge for one kind of product goes in that product's category: Philips Hue is Lighting.
- Generic building blocks, like MQTT, RESTful, or Modbus, go in DIY & makers.
- Helpers go in Helpers first. Add a real-world category only when the helper is clearly about one, like the utility meter, which is also Energy.
- A weather station measures the weather, it doesn't forecast it. Use Weather & alerts on its own, and keep Forecasts for services that actually forecast.

Entity and system integrations, like `light` or `recorder`, are not something people browse for and have no categories. Neither do virtual integrations: they show the categories of the integration they point to.

## All categories

Every category below lists the values you can put in `categories`. Some categories have subcategories, to narrow it down: use the category on its own when your integration covers more of it, and the subcategory when one fits. A smart thermostat is `climate.thermostats_heating`. A brand that makes thermostats, air conditioners, and fans is `climate`.

Find a category by what your integration is for, like "thermostat" or "pool", or jump to one.
